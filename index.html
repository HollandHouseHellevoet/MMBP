The Independence Tax

Independent employers pay a structural penalty for staying independent.

  • Physician practices, physician-owned hospitals, churches, and community banks send millions per year to insurance carriers across health, workers comp, med mal, and property and casualty
  • Carriers retain 15 to 30 percent as margin, invest the premium float, and book the underwriting surplus as equity on their balance sheets. UnitedHealth holds $25.5 billion of it. Elevance holds $40.3 billion.
  • Large employers eliminated this cost decades ago by self-funding. Small employers cannot, because they lack the infrastructure.
  • This is not an insurance problem. It is a capital allocation problem.

The founder's line: "We do not buy practices. We make them unbuyable."

How Money Moves

Premiums arrive at the beginning of the policy period. Claims are paid 30, 60, 90 days later. In that gap, carriers hold the money and invest it. The returns belong to the carrier. Warren Buffett built Berkshire Hathaway on this mechanic. MedMerge redirects it.

MedMerge bears underwriting risk by design. Float and surplus belong to MedMerge, not the members. Four revenue streams compound with scale: PEPM platform fees, float (investment income on premium during claims lag), underwriting surplus (retained margin after claims), and vendor relationship economics.

At maturity: billions in premium under management, hundreds of millions in retained surplus, permanent capital base, network effects that widen with time. PE buys the asset.
